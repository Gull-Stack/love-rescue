# Draft Organizing Documents — Love Rescue Foundation

Drafts for the three governance documents the formation requires. Items in `[BRACKETS]` need
founder input. **Have a nonprofit attorney in your state review before filing** — states add
their own required provisions (registered agent format, member/no-member election, etc.).

---

## 1. Articles of Incorporation — required IRS clauses

The IRS *organizational test* fails without an acceptable purpose clause and dissolution
clause. This language tracks the IRS's published sample (Pub. 557):

### Purpose clause

> The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable and educational purposes within the
> meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or the corresponding section of
> any future federal tax code, including, without limitation, to provide relationship
> education, skills training, and restorative support services that strengthen marriages,
> families, and interpersonal relationships among individuals and families who are
> underserved in their communities, with services offered without regard to ability to pay.

### Prohibited-activities clause

> No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be
> distributable to, its members, trustees, officers, or other private persons, except that
> the corporation shall be authorized and empowered to pay reasonable compensation for
> services rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of the purposes set
> forth herein. No substantial part of the activities of the corporation shall be the
> carrying on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting to influence legislation, and the
> corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the publishing or
> distribution of statements) any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any
> candidate for public office.

### Dissolution clause

> Upon the dissolution of the corporation, assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt
> purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or the
> corresponding section of any future federal tax code, or shall be distributed to the
> federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose.

## 2. Bylaws (outline — expand with state counsel)

1. **Name & Purpose** — restate the purpose clause.
2. **Board of Directors** — number (recommend 3–7); staggered `[1–3]`-year terms; powers;
   removal and vacancy procedures.
3. **Officers** — President/Chair, Secretary, Treasurer (many states require these three;
   one person usually may not hold both President and Secretary).
4. **Meetings** — annual meeting; regular meetings `[quarterly]`; special meetings; notice
   requirements; quorum = majority; action by unanimous written consent; remote participation
   permitted.
5. **Committees** — board may establish committees (e.g., finance, programs).
6. **Conflict of Interest** — incorporate the policy below by reference.
7. **Compensation** — directors serve without compensation (expense reimbursement allowed);
   officer/staff compensation must be reasonable and board-approved.
8. **Fiscal year** — `[January 1 – December 31]`.
9. **Indemnification** — to the fullest extent permitted by state law.
10. **Dissolution** — mirror the articles' dissolution clause.
11. **Amendments** — by `[two-thirds]` vote of the board.

## 3. Conflict-of-Interest Policy (IRS-model summary)

Adopt at the first board meeting. Based on the IRS sample policy in the Form 1023 instructions:

1. **Purpose** — protect the corporation when it contemplates a transaction that might
   benefit the private interest of an officer or director.
2. **Duty to disclose** — interested persons must disclose the existence and nature of any
   financial interest to the board.
3. **Recusal & determination** — the interested person leaves the room; remaining directors
   determine whether a more advantageous arrangement is reasonably available; a conflicted
   transaction may be approved only by a majority of disinterested directors finding it fair
   and in the corporation's best interest.
4. **Records** — minutes must record disclosures, recusals, and votes.
5. **Annual statements** — each director/officer annually affirms receipt and understanding
   of the policy.
6. **Periodic reviews** — compensation and vendor arrangements reviewed for reasonableness.

> **Love Rescue–specific note:** if the nonprofit will use, license, or pay for the existing
> Love Rescue app or services from a founder-owned company, that is a classic related-party
> transaction. It's allowed, but it must be disclosed, approved by disinterested directors,
> priced at or below fair market value, and documented — otherwise it risks *private
> inurement*, which is an absolute bar to 501(c)(3) status and grounds for revocation and
> excise taxes.

## First board meeting agenda (adopt-everything meeting)

1. Elect officers.
2. Adopt bylaws.
3. Adopt conflict-of-interest policy; collect signed annual statements.
4. Authorize opening a bank account (banks will want the EIN + articles + this resolution).
5. Authorize the officer to obtain the EIN and file Form 1023-EZ/1023 and pay the user fee.
6. Approve initial budget and program plan (reuse `CURRICULUM-16-WEEKS.md` as the core program).
7. Record minutes; Secretary signs.
